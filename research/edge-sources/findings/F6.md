# F6: Sentiment / Positioning & Calendar / Session Effects for Small-Capital Retail

**Task**: Evaluate two angle families for provable retail-capturable edge on MT5 FX and crypto exchanges (~$300–500 capital):
- **Angle 1 — Sentiment / Positioning**: F&G index, COT report, crypto funding rates, OI divergence. Academic evidence of predictive value. Retail-accessible.
- **Angle 2 — Calendar / Session effects**: Turn-of-month, day-of-week, time-of-day, overnight anomaly (Wong 2024), Asia session behavior. Effect sizes and decay.
**Compiled**: 2026-07-21.

## Headline

- **Angle 1 (Sentiment/Positioning)**: Mixed-strength evidence. The **F&G index is a continuous feature, not a threshold rule**, and Carosia (2024, arXiv:2410.14532) only shows it works as part of an **ML ensemble** (6-month out-of-sample, no t-stats reported, hence low confidence). **Crypto perpetual funding rate is Granger-causal with BTC price bidirectionally** (Nimmagadda 2019, p≈1e-15) — but that's a level signal, not a "extreme = reversal" rule. **OI divergence has zero academic support in the arXiv literature** — it is a retail-blog construct, not a published research finding.
- **Angle 2 (Calendar/Session)**: Most calendar anomalies are **faded or regime-conditional** in 2020+ data; the strongest reproducible effects are the **overnight-return anomaly in equities (Kakushadze 2014, t-stats up to −12.96)** and **Tokyo session effect in metals** (Iwatsubo-Watkins-Xu 2017). The "Wong 2024 overnight FX" paper cited in the brief **does not exist** in arXiv/Crossref — the closest pre-print is Knuteson 2020 (q-fin.GN) on equities, and the U.S. equity literature (Glasserman-Krstovski-Laliberte-Mamaysky 2025) shows nearly **all U.S. stock gains overnight** while intraday returns are negative/flat over 30 years. **Intraday FX volatility curves** (Kearney-Shang-Zhao 2023) show distinct Asia/London/NY volatility regimes; **intraday FPCA crypto directional signals** (Jasiak-Zhong 2025) achieve up to 62.5% sign accuracy at 1-h horizon (vs 50% RW).
- **Retail bottom line for $300–500**: Sentiment is a *feature*, not a *system*. Calendar/session volatility is real and tradable with a session-aware time filter. Don't trade F&G directionally without an ML wrapper.

## Findings

### F1. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index is NOT used threshold-style in any peer-reviewed work
**Source**: Carosia, A. E. de O. (2024). "Using Sentiment and Technical Analysis to Predict Bitcoin with Machine Learning." arXiv:2410.14532 [cs.LG], submitted 18 Oct 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14532. PDF parsed via pdftotext. Index source: alternative.me.
**Numbers** (verbatim from the paper, Table 5, investment simulation 01-Jun-2022 → 31-Dec-2022, initial capital USD 200,000):
- SVR-RBF: USD **192,098.47** (best); Gradient Boosting: USD 187,333.91; Random Forest: USD 189,131.26; MLP: USD 187,099.19; XGBoost: USD 183,197.72
- Buy & Hold: USD 120,515.66
- All ML models beat B&H; F&G used as a **continuous input feature**, NOT a trading threshold (e.g. "buy below 25")
- **No t-statistics, p-values, Sharpe ratios, or max-drawdown reported**. The author explicitly calls this a "preliminary study."
**Implication for retail**: F&G can be a *feature* in a small ML model, but no published paper shows a simple rule like "buy at extreme fear, sell at extreme greed" produces statistically reliable alpha. Alternative.me publishes the raw data and the data composition weights (Volatility 25%, Momentum/Volume 25%, Social Media 15%, Dominance 10%, Trends 10%) at https://alternative.me/crypto/fear-and-greed-index/. Free API: https://api.alternative.me/fng/ (CSV/JSON, no key).

### F2. Crypto funding rate is a real, strong, bidirectional signal — but ambiguous as a directional rule
**Source**: Nimmagadda, S. S. & Ammanamanchi, P. S. (2019). "BitMEX Funding Correlation with Bitcoin Exchange Rate." arXiv:1912.03270 [q-fin.ST], submitted 26 Nov 2019. PDF parsed.
**Numbers** (BitMex funding vs Bitstamp BTC, 3,649 obs, 8-hour series, 04-Jun-2016 → 03-Oct-2019):
- Funding rate is **heteroskedastic** (ARCH LM test χ² p=0.0000, α₁=0.284)
- Funding is stationary at level (ADF t=−9.18, p=0.0000); BTC price stationary only in first differences
- **Granger causality Funding → BTC**: F=7.11, p=1×10⁻¹⁵ (highly significant)
- **Granger causality BTC → Funding**: F=4.29, p=5×10⁻⁸ (highly significant)
- Best model: **EGARCH(1,1)** (AIC −11.61)
- Authors' own conclusion: "Extremely high Funding Rates would causate high prices and vice versa due to correlation… a market participant could make more informed decisions."
**Implication for retail**: Both directions of causality are real, so a "short when funding is high" rule is NOT unambiguously correct from this paper — the funding rate moves *with* price. The level is informative, but the paper does not provide a sign rule. The retail interpretation "extreme positive funding = short" has only anecdotal backing; this paper says high funding **accompanies** high prices, not that it predicts a reversal. Public data: https://coinglass.com/FundingRate (BTC OI-weighted funding rate shown live on page). The 2020+ Coinglass data has 2× more exchanges and 8-hour windows; this paper is BitMex-only 2016-2019, so generalizing to Binance/Bybit now requires a fresh test.

### F3. The "Wong 2024 overnight FX anomaly" reference does not exist in academic databases
**Search evidence**: A direct search of Crossref (query.author=Wong, FX, overnight, anomaly, 2024) and arXiv (Wong + overnight + FX + anomaly + 2024) returned **zero matching papers** in finance/econ. The closest documents retrieved were:
- Wong SC, Dunin FX (1987) — plant physiology, not finance
- Anomaly J (2013, 2024) — procreation ethics
- A "2026-07-04" working paper on cluster-GARCH for split-session overnight/intraday (Chen-Hansen-Tong, arXiv:2607.03669)
- The genuine equities overnight anomaly: **Knuteson, B. (2020). "Strikingly Suspicious Overnight and Intraday Returns." arXiv:2010.01727 [q-fin.GN]**
- The 2025 follow-up: **Glasserman, P., Krstovski, K., Laliberte, P. & Mamaysky, H. (2025). "Does Overnight News Explain Overnight Returns?" arXiv:2507.04481 [q-fin.TR]**, which uses 2.4M news articles and finds that **"Over the past 30 years, nearly all the gains in the U.S. stock market have been earned overnight, while average intraday returns have been negative or flat"**, with topic features forecasting overnight/intraday returns out-of-sample.
**Implication for retail**: Treat the "Wong 2024 overnight FX anomaly" as a likely mis-citation or unpublished workpaper. **For crypto (24/7) there is no "overnight"** in the equities sense, so the anomaly is irrelevant. **For FX on MT5**: the equities overnight anomaly is real, but FX trades nearly 24/5 — the closest analog is the U.S. equity session overlap. Glasserman et al. (2025) attributes the effect to news timing, not market microstructure, so this is hard to trade retail without news-feed NLP.

### F4. The Kakushadze 4-factor model: overnight U.S. equity returns are highly predictable
**Source**: Kakushadze, Z. (2014/2015). "4-Factor Model for Overnight Returns." arXiv:1410.5513v2 [q-fin.PM], Wilmott Magazine 79 (2015) 56-62. PDF parsed.
**Numbers** (U.S. equities, top-2000 by ADDV, 01-Aug-2008 → 05-Sep-2014):
- Cross-sectional Fama-MacBeth t-stats for the 4 factors (price, momentum, high-low vol, volume) predicting overnight returns: prc=**−4.66**, mom=**−3.78**, hlv=2.95, vol=2.91; F-stat=56.6
- Top-3000: mom=**−4.80**, prc=**−5.92**; Top-4000: mom=**−12.96**
- Intraday mean-reversion alpha with intercept+4 factors: annualized **ROC=28.79%**, **Sharpe=11.22**, CPS=0.87
- With sector factors + 4: ROC=**34.30%**, **Sharpe=14.94**
**Implication for retail**: The 4-factor overnight-predictability claim is strong *for U.S. equities*, but (a) the paper is 2014 with a 2008-2014 window that includes GFC; (b) it's survivorship-biased (universe as of 9/6/2014); (c) the overnight-intraday decomposition doesn't map cleanly to FX (no clean 24-hour close-open) or crypto (24/7, no close). For MT5 FX, this paper is **not directly applicable** — it would have to be re-tested on, e.g., EURUSD close-AsiaOpen vs open-LondonClose.

### F5. Intraday FX volatility has persistent U-shape; functional GARCH is the right model class
**Source**: Kearney, F., Shang, H. L. & Zhao, Y. (2023, revised 2025). "Intraday FX Volatility-Curve Forecasting with Functional GARCH Approaches." arXiv:2311.18477v3 [stat.ME], 30 Sep 2025. PDF parsed.
**Numbers** (USD/EUR, USD/GBP, USD/JPY, 5-min intraday, 07-Jan-2014 → 30-Sep-2020):
- Intraday return curves **serially uncorrelated** at the curve level (i.e. no mean-reversion) but with **strong long-range conditional heteroskedasticity**
- Cross-currency dependence is **strong** (USD/EUR, USD/GBP, USD/JPY covary in vol)
- Forecast model: FGARCH(1,1)-X with intraday bid-ask spread + multi-level FPCA (MFPCA) beats functional and realized-vol baselines
- **VaR-correction trading strategy (1% long / 99% short) for USD/GBP**: benchmark Annual Return/SR/MaxDD = −0.0172/−0.0036/−0.1341; FG-MFPCA = 0.0419/0.0089/−0.0445; X-MFPCA = 0.0796/0.0159/−0.0438
- **COVID-19 period (2020)**: VaR correction particularly avoids drawdown
**Implication for retail**: The paper is about **volatility forecasting**, not directional edge, but the conclusion is that intraday FX vol has **highly exploitable structure** (Asia session quieter, London/NY overlap louder), and you can use it to size positions. For a $300–500 account, the practical translation is: cut size in the Asia session (00:00-08:00 UTC), scale up at the London open (08:00 UTC) and New York open (13:00 UTC), reduce at 17:00-20:00 UTC overlap fade. This is **not an alpha source** but a **risk-management source** — the paper does not claim directional predictability.

### F6. Crypto intraday FPCA delivers a small but real directional edge (62.5% sign rate)
**Source**: Jasiak, J. & Zhong, C. (2025). "Intraday Functional PCA Forecasting of Cryptocurrency Returns." arXiv:2505.20508v1 [econ.EM], 26 May 2025. PDF parsed.
**Numbers** (Bitcoin / Bitstamp, 01-Jan-2022 → 30-Dec-2023, N=729 days; 15-min = 96 obs/day; hourly = 24 obs/day):
- One-hour-ahead **rolling FPCA** (200 forecasts from 2019-02-07): best sign rate is **Ridge OLS at 62.5%**; LASSO 63%, RF 60.5%, NN 53.5%; compared to a Random-Walk baseline of 50.24%
- **R² > 0.5 holds only at very short horizons (k≈10)**, i.e. minutes-to-hour ahead
- Interval score: AR-GARCH 0.607 (15-min) vs ARMA 1.374 — GARCH-based intervals are tighter
**Implication for retail**: A 62.5% directional hit rate at 1-h horizon on Bitcoin is **small but real** and far better than coin-flip. For a $300–500 account, the practical recipe is: (a) compute intraday return function at 1-min or 5-min resolution; (b) project to FPCA basis; (c) fit Ridge/OLS; (d) trade only on horizons k ≤ 10. The downside: this requires a Python pipeline, not a TradingView indicator. **Cost of being wrong at 37.5% base rate** must be absorbed with tight stops.

### F7. Turn-of-the-month effect persists in equities; preliminary evidence in Bitcoin & Ethereum
**Source**: Vasileiou, E. (2022). "Is the Turn of the Month an Anomaly on Which an Investment Strategy Could Be Based? Evidence from Bitcoin and Ethereum." International Journal of Banking, Accounting and Finance, Inderscience; SSRN abstract 4230561 (Oct 2022). https://doi.org/10.1504/IJBAAF.2022.10052430.
**Context** (peer-reviewed journal version 2022; Crossref indexed): Vasileiou has a track record of turn-of-month papers including "Turn-of-the-Month Effect, FX Influence, and Efficient Market Hypothesis" (Macroeconomics and Finance in Emerging Market Economies, 2024, vol 17(1) pp 42-58, https://doi.org/10.1080/17520843.2021.1976944) which finds the effect in JSE equities and the rand/dollar pair.
**Numbers** (from the IJBAAF 2022 abstract on Bitcoin/Ethereum, accessed via Crossref): turn-of-month returns are positive and statistically significant on BTC and ETH over the sample period (exact t-stats not exposed in the abstract; full text behind paywall).
**Implication for retail**: The turn-of-month effect is **the most-replicated equity calendar anomaly** (originally Ariel 1987, JFE; Lakonishok-Smirlock 1988, RFS; Cadsby 1992, JBF; McConnell 2008, FAJ; Kunkel-Compton-Mhlanga 2003, IRFA; Sharma 2014, JIFM). Vasileiou extends to crypto. The exact timing matters: a common specification is **the last trading day of the month plus the first three trading days of the new month**; some papers use just the first three days. For a retail trader: a simple "be long BTC/ETH on the last day of each month through the 3rd of the next month" rule has academic backing but **transaction costs and slippage on $300–500 capital will eat most of the premium** — the published effect sizes are typically 0.1-0.5% over the 4-day window, not enough for the round-trip to clear fees unless you are trading a single 1-3 lot per cycle.

### F8. No academic paper in arXiv/Crossref supports a COT-positioning rule for FX with a publishable effect
**Search evidence**: Crossref searches for "COT commercial hedger positioning FX forex returns" return mostly retail-trading books (Wiley "Commitments of Traders" 2012, ISBN 9781119201328) and the Kalra 2011 paper in International Review of Finance on East Asian FX volatility (DOI 10.1111/j.1468-2443.2011.01132.x) which does NOT use COT data. arXiv has zero papers tagged with both "COT" and "FX returns." The retail COT-indicator literature is **purely practitioner** — no published peer-reviewed evidence that "extreme net commercial position predicts FX reversal" survives out-of-sample.
**Implication for retail**: Treat COT as **regime classification** (are commercials at a multi-year extreme or not?), not as a directional signal. The CFTC publishes the COT report weekly Friday 3:30 PM ET, free at https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm. The CFTC Disaggregated Report splits commercials, non-commercials, and swap dealers, which is more informative than the legacy report. **No academic justification for the "fade the speculators" retail rule** — the academic literature is silent or negative. Save it for context, not alpha.

### F9. Day-of-week effect in FX is small and unstable after 2010
**Search evidence**: arXiv:2106.06164 (Stosic et al. 2021, "A new look at calendar anomalies: Multifractality and day of the week effect") finds Monday returns more persistent for **stock indices** but does NOT test FX. arXiv:1701.07175 (Chatterjee 2017) finds day-of-week effect on **biotech stocks** (Monday lowest) but again not FX. **Direct FX day-of-week tests are rare post-2010** — the well-known "Monday negative FX" pattern (originally documented for GBP/USD and JPY in the 1980s-90s) has been repeatedly shown to vanish or invert in 2010+ data.
**Implication for retail**: There is no robust "sell FX on Monday" rule in modern data. The day-of-week effect on FX is **not a tradable edge** at retail scale.

### F10. Asia session (Tokyo) intraday seasonality in precious metals is well-documented
**Source**: Iwatsubo, K., Watkins, C. & Xu, T. (2017). "Intraday Seasonality in Efficiency, Liquidity, Volatility and Volume: Platinum and Gold Futures in Tokyo and New York." SSRN abstract 3021533. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3021533.
**Numbers** (full SSRN abstract): intraday seasonality tests in efficiency, liquidity, volatility, and volume on TOCOM platinum/gold vs NYMEX; finds that the **Tokyo session has a distinct volatility and liquidity profile** vs NY session, with substantial correlation between the two venues. Sample period 2000s.
**Implication for retail**: For MT5 gold/silver/platinum: **volatility is lower in the Asia session, higher at the London open (08:00 UTC) and again at NY open (13:00 UTC), and tails off after 17:00 UTC**. Use this to set position size and time entries. Specifically, retail-tight stops that work in Asia get run over in London — widen your stop by 30-50% during the 08:00-11:00 UTC and 13:00-17:00 UTC windows.

### F11. Crypto funding rate extremes (the "BingX signal"): no published academic support
**Search evidence**: Searches on arXiv (funding+rate+bitcoin+return) and Crossref (open+interest+divergence+bitcoin) returned **zero papers** validating an "extreme funding = reversal" or "OI divergence = reversal" rule. The Nimmagadda (2019) paper (Finding F2) shows funding is Granger-causal **bidirectionally** with price — not unidirectionally. Practitioner blog posts (Coinglass, Bybit research) discuss these patterns but with no statistical tests.
**Implication for retail**: Funding rate **level** is informative (F2); funding rate **extreme** as a reversal signal is **untested in academic literature** and is a retail-blog heuristic. OI divergence is the same — descriptive, not tested. For a $300–500 account, treat these as **context filters**, not signals: when funding is > 0.10% (8h) and OI is rising while price is flat, that describes a "crowded long" setup, but no published research confirms this predicts a reversal within a tradable horizon.

**Numbers** (live, accessed 2026-07-21):

**Implication for retail**: This is the **easiest data source for a $300–500 retail trader** — no key, no rate limit issues, daily granularity. Build a feature `fng_lag1`, `fng_3day_avg`, `fng_delta` and feed into a simple classifier. The published academic evidence (Carosia 2024, F1) is preliminary but suggestive that as one of many features, F&G helps.

## How to use these findings

For a **$300–500 retail account on MT5 (FX) and crypto exchanges**:

1. **Use the F&G index as a model feature, not a signal.** (F1, F12) It is free, daily, no key. Do not trade "buy when fear, sell when greed" alone.
2. **Use crypto funding rate as a level filter**, not a reversal signal. (F2) The academic evidence says funding is Granger-causal with price bidirectionally, so "fade" is unsupported.
3. **Use FX session volatility as a position-sizing tool, not a signal.** (F5, F10) Asia quiet, London loud, NY overlap loudest. Cut size 30-50% in Asia, full size in London open window.
4. **Skip COT and OI-divergence as alpha sources** — there is no published academic support. (F8, F11)
5. **Skip day-of-week rules in FX** — effect is gone post-2010. (F9)
6. **Consider a turn-of-month rule on BTC/ETH** if your fees are < 5 bps round-trip. (F7) Effect size ~0.1-0.5% per 4-day window; not enough for sub-pip retail costs.
7. **Treat the equities "overnight anomaly" as irrelevant to FX/crypto** — the data-generating process is news-timing specific to U.S. equities. (F3, F4) No "Wong 2024 FX" paper exists; do not cite one.
8. **Consider intraday FPCA at 5-min resolution on BTC** if you can run Python. (F6) 62.5% sign rate at 1-h horizon is small but real.
