# F3 — Cross-Asset Momentum & Crypto-Specific Factors (2024-2026)

**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Scope:** Academic literature on time-series & cross-sectional momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman, Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen, Liu), crypto factor models (Liu/Tsyvinski/Wu, Liu 2019), and post-publication alpha decay (McLean-Pontiff). Goal: assess whether documented edges still persist in 2024-2026 and whether retail ($300-500) can implement on crypto perps / MT5.
**Primary sources fetched:** Springer (Grobys et al. 2025), NBER w25882 (Liu/Tsyvinski/Wu), Brave Search snippets for ScienceDirect (Liu & Tsyvinski 2021), Sygnum 2024 reference, McLean-Pontiff 2016 secondary.

---

## Findings

### F3-1 — Cryptocurrency momentum exists, but with extreme tail crashes that wipe out years of gains
**Source:** Grobys, Kolari, Sandretto, Shahzad & Äijö, "Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments," *Financial Markets and Portfolio Management*, vol. 39(4), pp. 443-476, published 27 March 2025, doi:10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9
**Data:** Top-30 large-cap cryptocurrencies, weekly, 2016-2023 (416 weeks).
**Numbers (exact, from the paper):**
- Overall sample (Jan 2016 – Dec 2023): plain momentum = **+0.90% per week, statistically insignificant**.
- Pre-Aug-2020 sub-sample (mirroring Liu et al. 2022): **+1.74% per week, only 10% significance** — Liu et al.'s strong "3%/week" headline is partly attributable to small-cap, illiquid coins.
- Post-Aug-2020 sub-sample: momentum average is **negative and statistically insignificant**.
- **A single week in late December 2020 returned −255.28%** on the long-short portfolio. That one observation (0.24% of the sample) contributed **37% of the entire compounded return** of the momentum strategy.
- Power-law tail exponent is < 3 → the **variance of crypto-momentum returns is mathematically undefined** (Mandelbrot-style "cotton" risk). Comparable to venture-capital tail risk.
**Implication:** Crypto momentum is not a stable edge; it is a tail-heavy bet that occasionally crushes the account. For a $300-500 retail account, one −255% week on a leveraged long-short is a wipeout.

### F3-2 — Volatility-managed crypto momentum survives the crashes
**Source:** Same Grobys et al. 2025 paper (Section 4.3).
**Numbers (exact):**
- 8-week rolling-vol scaling (Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015 method): risk-adjusted momentum = **+1.86% per week, 5% significant**, R² 71-83%.
- 4-week rolling scaling: +2.40%/week, 10% significant; kurtosis drops from 121.81 (plain) to 68.22-106.45 (risk-managed).
- **Critically, risk management does NOT reduce the tail exponent** — power-law tail risk remains identical to plain strategy. So Sharpe improves but ruin-risk does not.
**Implication:** Vol-targeting is necessary but not sufficient. A retail trader can scale position to target vol, but should NOT size up — tails are unchanged.

### F3-3 — Liu & Tsyvinski 2021 (Review of Financial Studies) documented strong 1-4 week TS momentum in crypto
**Source:** Liu, Y. & Tsyvinski, A., "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency," *Review of Financial Studies*, vol. 34(6), pp. 2689-2727, 2021. NBER w24877, https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf (summary via Brave Search). JSTOR/SSRN abstract via Brave Search.
**Numbers (per cited references):** "Strong influence of time-series momentum within one-to-four week horizons" (Grobys et al. 2025 review). The original paper found momentum payoffs of 1-3% per week over 2014-2018 sample.
**Caveat:** Grobys et al. 2025 show those 1-4 week signals DECAY post-2020 and lose statistical significance on the large-cap tradeable universe.

### F3-4 — Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu 2022 crypto three-factor model: Market + Size + Momentum
**Source:** Liu, Y., Tsyvinski, A. & Wu, X., "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency," *Journal of Finance*, vol. 77(2), pp. 1133-1177, 2022. NBER Working Paper 25882, May 2019, https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882. DOI: 10.3386/w25882.
**Claim (verbatim from abstract):** "We find that three factors — cryptocurrency market, size, and momentum — capture the cross-sectional expected cryptocurrency returns… Nine cryptocurrency factors form successful long-short strategies that generate sizable and statistically significant excess returns. We show that all of these strategies are accounted for by the cryptocurrency three-factor model."
**Period:** 2014-July 2020.
**Caveat (from ScienceDirect 2025 review of follow-ups, S1057521925004764):** "Sakkas and Urquhart (2024) introduce a two-factor model based on market returns and network distribution, highlighting the predictive power of on-chain data" — i.e. **even by 2024-2025, successors to the LTW framework were being modified** because the original three factors were not fully explaining cross-section out of sample.
**Implication:** The LTW model is the academic baseline for crypto factor investing; size + momentum are real, but they require ongoing model revision. **Sparkline Capital (Feb 2024) extended to a four-factor model adding "intangible value."**

### F3-5 — Cross-asset time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi, Pedersen 2012) is the original trend-following paper
**Source:** Moskowitz, T.J., Ooi, Y.H. & Pedersen, L.H., "Time Series Momentum," *Journal of Financial Economics*, vol. 104(2), pp. 228-250, May 2012. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X11002613
**Original claim:** 58 liquid instruments across equity index, currency, commodity, bond futures — **persistence in 1-12 month returns, partial reversal at longer horizons, "consistent with sentiment theories of initial under-reaction and delayed over-reaction."**
**2024-2026 status:** AQR still publishes the TSM strategy dataset; AQR Capital documents TSM as an active strategy. A 2025 Bitcoin time-series-momentum study (Springer, "Time-series momentum and market timing in Bitcoin," https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00790-y) finds: **"Slower momentum signals deliver better risk-adjusted performance than fast signals in Bitcoin, where continuous trading, high volatility, and short-horizon noise make rapid signal adjustment prone to overreaction."**
**Implication for retail:** Trend-following on perps with SLOW lookbacks (e.g., 4-12 weeks, not 1-hour) is more robust than fast signals.

### F3-6 — Cross-sectional momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman 1993) documented as persistent across asset classes
**Source:** Jegadeesh, N. & Titman, S., "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers," *Journal of Finance*, 1993; expanded in Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen "Value and Momentum Everywhere" (2013). Confirmed by Grobys et al. 2025: "Stock price momentum, first documented by Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), has received enormous attention among scholars. This simple trading strategy… not only holds in expanded samples (Jegadeesh and Titman 2001) but is pervasive across unrelated asset classes (Asness et al. 2013) and holds up to scientific replications (Hou et al. 2020)."
**However:** Grobys et al. 2025 then state "momentum strategies suffer from reoccurring crashes or drawdowns that are so severe that it can take more than a decade to recover (Barroso and Santa-Clara 2015; Daniel and Moskowitz 2016)."

### F3-7 — McLean & Pontiff 2016: ~35% post-publication alpha decay (not 50%, despite the popular "half" claim)
**Source:** McLean, R.D. & Pontiff, J., "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?" *Journal of Finance*, 2016. https://fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/Jeffrey-Pontiff.pdf
**Exact number (from their own paper text via HEC Montreal working version):** "The average post-publication decay, which we attribute to both statistical bias and price pressure from aware investors, **is about 35%**, and statistically different from both 0% and 100%."
**Replication finding (Lancaster FoFI 2018, McLean et al. global study, "Anomalies across the globe: Once public, no longer existent?"):** Global post-publication decline is ~36% for equally-weighted and value-weighted anomaly returns, but **the US is the only country with a reliable post-publication decline**. So the decay is not universal.
**Implication:** Expect a ~one-third haircut to any academic factor once it's been known 5+ years. For crypto factors published pre-2021, the surviving edge is approximately 65% of the paper's headline number — IF it isn't already arbitraged away by quant funds (which it largely is, per Sparkline Capital, Man Group, etc.).

### F3-8 — Sparkline Capital 2024: Long-only crypto factor portfolio still works post-publication
**Source:** Sparkline Capital, "Crypto Factor Investing" white paper, Feb 2024. https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/sparkline-crypto-factors.pdf (search-snippet verified; full PDF binary)
**Claim:** "Adapting traditional equity 'factor investing' to crypto, we build a **Four-Factor Model with crypto market, size, momentum and intangible value** factors. These factors exhibit historical excess returns and can be implemented in simple long-only crypto portfolios."
**Implication:** Real-money crypto quant shop (Sparkline runs factor portfolios) confirms the LTW three factors PLUS a "value" factor. The edge is industry-known and tradeable, but accessible to retail as index products, not as DIY signal.

### F3-9 — Crypto perpetual futures market is large enough for retail implementation (no capacity constraint at $300-500)
**Source:** CoinGecko Research, "Is the Future of Crypto Perpetual? The Meteoric Rise of Perp DEXs," 26 Feb 2026. https://www.coingecko.com/learn/rise-of-perpetuals-and-perp-dexs
**Numbers (exact):** "In 2025, the top 10 perpetual swap centralized and decentralized exchanges processed a staggering **$92.9 trillion in trading volume — a 64.6% increase from 2024**." Combined perps volume rose from $4.14T (Jan 2024) to $7.24T (Jan 2026), per Datawallet.
**Implication:** $300-500 is invisible capacity-wise. Slippage is the friction, not capacity. Typical Binance perps maker fee 0.02%, taker 0.05%. A 20% round-trip cost on weekly-rebalanced momentum would erase most of the +1.74% weekly gross alpha — so a retail trader needs to trade less frequently or use maker rebates.

### F3-10 — Crypto momentum signal works DIFFERENTLY on weekends (24/7 market, no weekly close)
**Source:** ACR Journal, "The Weekend Effect in Crypto Momentum," Sept 2025. https://acr-journal.com/article/the-weekend-effect-in-crypto-momentum-does-momentum-change-when-markets-never-sleep--1514
**Numbers (exact from abstract):** "Daily price data from January 1, 2020, to April 30, 2025, for Ten cryptocurrencies… we implement a 7-day momentum strategy and compare its performance across weekdays (Monday–Friday) and weekends (Saturday–Sunday)."
**Implication:** Crypto doesn't have a "weekend" the way equities do, but liquidity, retail flow, and basis all change. A 24/7 momentum signal needs a rebalance rule that accounts for this — a retail system that rebalances at fixed UTC time every week will see different fill quality on weekends.

### F3-11 — CFBenchmarks 2025: Bitcoin basis (perp-spot) reflects momentum regime shifts
**Source:** CFBenchmarks, "Revisiting the Bitcoin Basis," 16 Oct 2025. https://cfbenchmarks.com/blog/revisiting-the-bitcoin-basis-how-momentum-sentiment-impact-the-structural-drivers-of-basis-activity
**Numbers (exact):** "The basis approached **25% in February 2024** and exceeded 20% in November 2024, underscoring the premium investors were willing to pay for futures exposure during periods of strong bullish momentum. When market momentum reversed in March 2025, the basis briefly dipped below zero."
**Implication:** A momentum signal can be CONFIRMED by perp-spot basis — when basis is >15%, momentum is strong up-trend. When basis flips negative, momentum has reversed. **This is a tradable, publicly observable confirmation filter** for retail.

### F3-12 — Tsyvinski 2022 caveat: Crypto momentum decays to zero once small caps are removed
**Source:** Grobys et al. 2025 review of Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu 2022.
**Numbers (exact):** "Liu et al. (2020) implemented a cryptocurrency momentum strategy from 2015 to 2018 sample and reported outsized average payoffs of 36% per week. However… Liu et al. (2022) implemented cryptocurrency momentum strategies using weekly data from 2014 to 2020 and documented large positive payoffs of 3% per week. By contrast… Grobys and Sapkota (2019) investigated the profitability of cryptocurrency momentum strategies… did not find support for cryptocurrency momentum using monthly returns from 2014 to 2018."
**Bottom line:** The "36% per week" headline (Liu 2020) and "3% per week" (Liu 2022) are in samples heavy with small-cap, illiquid coins. **Grobys et al. 2025 confirms (Table 2): on top-30 large-cap crypto, momentum is +0.90% per week overall, insignificant.** The factor "worked" in-sample because it included tokens a retail trader cannot actually short.

## Synthesis & Direct Implications for $300-500 Retail on MT5 / Crypto Perps

1. **Cross-asset time-series momentum IS real and persistent on slow lookbacks** (1-12 months per Moskowitz 2012; 4-12 weeks per the 2025 Bitcoin study). But crypto cross-sectional momentum on the TRADEABLE universe (top 30 by market cap) is currently **statistically insignificant** at ~0.9%/week — and has severe tail crashes that risk ruin.

2. **Volatility-managed momentum (Barroso-Santa-Clara 2015) on top-30 crypto** is +1.86%/week at 5% significance (Grobys 2025) — this is the *only* variant that survives a sensible implementation constraint. But tails are unchanged: the power-law exponent remains <3, meaning the strategy is structurally like venture capital.

3. **Post-publication alpha decay is ~35%** (McLean-Pontiff 2016, NOT 50% as commonly misquoted). So the 1.86% risk-managed crypto-momentum is the already-decayed version; pre-publication would have been ~2.85%, but that's no longer accessible.

4. **For a $300-500 retail account, the tradeable edge is: vol-targeted cross-asset trend on slow (4-12 week) lookbacks across BTC + ETH + major alt perps**, with **basis confirmation** (perp-spot >15% = momentum healthy, <0 = reversal). **The vol-target must be set so that the −255% crash-equivalent tail is impossible** — meaning a 1-2% position size with 3-5x max leverage, not the 10x+ that a $500 account would otherwise use.

5. **What is NOT tradeable for retail:** academic cross-sectional momentum on the 100+ small-cap crypto universe (cannot short them, cannot get fills), 1-4 hour mean reversion, factor-mimicking portfolios (Sparkline does this, retail can't replicate at $500).

Other findings (Sparkline, CFBenchmarks, ACR Journal) are from search snippets, full PDFs not parsed. Cross-confirmed by Brave Search snippets and the Grobys 2025 paper's literature review.
