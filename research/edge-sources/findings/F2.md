# F2: Volatility Regime Detection + Volatility Risk Premium (Academic Sources)

## Angle 1: Volatility Regime Detection — GARCH, Markov-switching, HMM

### [F2-1] Single-regime GARCH is structurally misspecified on FX (Chaudhary 2026)
- Paper: "Multi-Scale Markov-Switching GARCH: Volatility Regime Detection in EUR/USD" (arXiv:2606.06190v1, submitted 4 Jun 2026, q-fin.ST). Author: Jayesh Chaudhary, Independent Researcher. Cites Hamilton (1989), Gray (1996), Haas/Mittnik/Paolella (2004), Filardo (1994), Bollerslev (1986).
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06190
- source_type: primary (arXiv working paper, full HTML available)
- confidence: medium-high (single-author independent study, but methodologically careful and well-cited)
- Key number: on EUR/USD 2021-2025, plain GARCH(1,1) yields α̂=0.3038, β̂=0.6076, persistence α̂+β̂=0.9114. The α̂ is 3-6× the typical 0.05-0.10 range for FX. Author describes this as the "hyper-reactive" signature of single-regime models absorbing regime-switching into the shock response.
- Implication: any GARCH-derived signal (ATR proxies, vol targeting) on FX should be flagged as regime-contaminated. Use MS-GARCH or at minimum two-state HMM filters to denoise.

### [F2-2] Three-state MS-GARCH produces statistically distinct regimes with monotonic vol ordering (Chaudhary 2026)
- Paper: same (arXiv:2606.06190v1).
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06190
- source_type: primary
- confidence: medium-high
- Key numbers: KS distributional purity test p=1.35×10⁻¹⁵³ (Calm vs Turbulent) on 31,152 OOS hourly EUR/USD observations. OOS annualised vols are monotonic: Calm 3.70% < Turbulent 10.92% < Crisis 24.72% (1H Micro). Brier Skill Score vs climatology: +0.2132 (1D), +0.0874 (4H), +0.1185 (1H).
- Implication: MS-GARCH cleanly separates three volatility regimes on FX. Calm/Crisis are not just vol levels but distinct data-generating processes (reject distributional null at extreme significance).

### [F2-3] MS-GARCH statistically beats GARCH(1,1) on volatility forecasting, but edge is thin (Chaudhary 2026)
- Paper: same (arXiv:2606.06190v1).
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06190
- source_type: primary
- confidence: medium-high
- Key numbers: Diebold-Mariano DM=+4.7040 (p=1.28×10⁻⁶) for MS-GARCH vs GARCH(1,1). Smoothed Spearman IC improves 0.5264 → 0.5371 (only +0.0107). RCM clarity: 27.6% (1D), 52.4% (4H), 54.0% (1H) — moderate, not high. OOS directional IC = +0.0252 (p=6.75×10⁻⁵).
- Implication: MS-GARCH is a real but modest improvement over GARCH. Author explicitly states the framework is a "risk management tool rather than a standalone alpha engine." For a $300-500 trader, the practical takeaway is regime-conditioned position sizing, not raw signal.

### [F2-4] TVTP (time-varying transition probabilities) decisively beats static Markov at intraday scales, not daily (Chaudhary 2026)
- Paper: same (arXiv:2606.06190v1).
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06190
- source_type: primary
- confidence: medium-high
- Key numbers: ΔAIC = +690.7 (4H) and +499.9 (1H) for TVTP over static Markov; 1D model is correctly static (positive AIC of +2170.24 suggests near-overparameterisation on 1,871 daily bars). Daily P[Calm→Crisis]≈0.001; hourly P[Calm→Turbulent]≈0.08.
- Implication: macro regime transitions are very rare per day (≈0.1%) but intraday turbulent episodes occur ~8% of hours. For an MT5/crypto trader using bar data: 1D MS-GARCH static is fine; intraday HFT-style HMM needs TVTP driven by microstructure stress indicators.

### [F2-5] Crude oil MS-GARCH beats single-regime GARCH in-sample and at daily frequency only (Zhang, Yao, He 2015)
- Paper: "Forecasting crude oil market volatility: can the Regime Switching GARCH model beat the single-regime GARCH models?" (arXiv:1512.01676, 5 Dec 2015, econ.GN / q-fin.ST, 30 pages).
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01676
- source_type: primary
- confidence: high
- Key findings: (1) Two-regime MRS-GARCH beats GARCH, GJR-GARCH, EGARCH in-sample under most criteria. (2) Two-regime MRS-GARCH provides more accurate vol forecasts for daily data but the superiority fades for weekly and monthly data. (3) Linear single-regime GARCH is best at Value-at-Risk forecasting. (4) Volatility forecast advantage of nonlinear GARCH (GJR, EGARCH) only appears at longer horizons.
- Implication: regime switching adds value at the trading frequency (daily/intraday), not for lower-frequency risk models. For an MT5 day-trader on crude oil futures proxy (USO, CL), MS-GARCH on daily bars is empirically justified.

### [F2-6] Bayesian nonparametric panel MS-GARCH finds cross-sectional regime clustering (Casarin, Costantini, Osuntuyi 2020)
- Paper: "Bayesian nonparametric panel Markov-switching GARCH models" (arXiv:2012.10124, 18 Dec 2020, stat.ME, 38 pages, 12 figures).
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10124
- source_type: primary
- confidence: medium-high
- Key findings: two-regime MS-GARCH on 78 S&P 100 constituents, 6 Jan 2000 – 3 Oct 2020 (~20 years). Soft parameter pooling via hierarchical Dirichlet + nonparametric clustering prior recovers true cluster counts in simulation; in S&P 100, finds 2 clusters in regime 1 and 3 clusters in regime 2 — i.e., stocks share volatility regimes but with distinct sub-structures.
- Implication: volatility regimes are stock-cluster-specific within broad market regimes. For a small-capital trader: a single MS-GARCH on a futures proxy may miss sector-specific vol clusters; mean-variance clustering within the broad market.

### [F2-7] Cryptocurrency volatility is best fit by skewed non-Gaussian GARCH distributions (Cerqueti, Giacalone, Mattera 2020)
- Paper: "Skewed non-Gaussian GARCH models for cryptocurrencies volatility modelling" (arXiv:2004.11674, 22 Apr 2020, q-fin.ST, 54 pages, 7 figures, 34 tables, accepted to Information Sciences).
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11674
- source_type: primary
- confidence: high (published in Information Sciences)
- Key findings: best forecast distribution is asset-specific — Skewed GED for BTC/USD and LTC/USD, skewed Student-t for ETH/USD. Comprehensive comparison across multiple GARCH variants.
- Implication: crypto vol is fat-tailed AND skewed; normal-GARCH underestimates tails. A practitioner selling crypto options (Deribit, Bybit options) needs to model skew explicitly, not just vol level. Practical on MT5: BTCUSD via MT5 has limited history; consider Deribit/Bybit vol surfaces for proper short-vega sizing.

### [F2-8] Filardo-style TVTP: transition probabilities depend on observable stress indicators, not just history (Filardo 1994, cited via Chaudhary 2026)
- Paper: Filardo, A. J. (1994). "Business-cycle phases and their transitional dynamics." Journal of Business & Economic Statistics, 12(3), 299-308. (Cited in arXiv:2606.06190.)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06190
- source_type: secondary (foundational paper, applied in 2026 paper)
- confidence: high
- Key concept: TVTP uses a multinomial logit on observable covariates (vol z-score, spread proxy, momentum) to predict regime shifts in advance, not merely detect them contemporaneously.
- Implication: a trader's regime filter should be a forward-looking logistic on stress indicators (VIX level + change, realised vol vs implied, credit spreads, breadth), not just a backward-looking Markov transition on past returns.

## Angle 2: Volatility Risk Premium and Options-Selling in Futures Proxy

### [F2-9] Realized GARCH gives a closed-form VRP and outperforms conventional GARCH on S&P 500 (Hansen, Huang, Tong, Wang 2021)
- Paper: "Realized GARCH, CBOE VIX, and the Volatility Risk Premium" (arXiv:2112.05302, 10 Dec 2021, econ.EM / q-fin.PR). Authors: Peter Reinhard Hansen, Zhuo Huang, Chen Tong, Tianyi Wang.
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05302
- source_type: primary
- confidence: high
- Key findings: Realized GARCH is driven by two shocks (return + volatility); the volatility shock enters the SDF and endows it with a volatility-risk compensation. The closed-form VIX and VRP from Realized GARCH significantly outperform conventional GARCH on S&P 500 returns + VIX + VRP.
- Implication: the VRP is a real, time-varying, modelable quantity. The negative of (implied vol minus realized vol) is the structural premium available to short-vega. The "volatility shock" component is what makes the premium non-trivial — it requires real-time realized vol as a separate state.

### [F2-10] Static VIX futures portfolios do NOT track VIX — only dynamic daily rebalancing does (Leung, Ward 2019)
- Paper: "Tracking VIX with VIX Futures: Portfolio Construction and Performance" (arXiv:1907.00293, 29 Jun 2019, q-fin.RM, 22 pages, book chapter). Authors: Tim Leung, Brian Ward.
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00293
- source_type: primary
- confidence: high
- Key finding (explicit negative result): "static portfolios of different VIX futures fail to track VIX closely. VIX futures simply do not react quickly enough to movements in the spot VIX." Only a discrete-time, daily-rebalanced dynamic strategy (calibrated to history, simulation study) tracks VIX better than the VXX ETN.
- Implication: passive short-vega (hold short front-month VIX future, roll) is structurally flawed — futures lag the spot VIX. A short-vega strategy on MT5 (via VIX-futures-tracking instruments like VXX, or synthetic VIX via S&P 500 options) MUST be dynamic, with intraday or daily rebalancing. The static "harvest contango" trade is documented to underperform dynamic strategies and the VXX ETN.

### [F2-11] Markov-modeled term structure of VIX futures yields a regime-dependent long/short signal (Avellaneda, Li, Papanicolaou, Wang 2021)
- Paper: "Trading Signals In VIX Futures" (arXiv:2103.02016v3, 23 Nov 2021, q-fin.CP / q-fin.TR / stat.ML). Authors: M. Avellaneda, T. N. Li, A. Papanicolaou, G. Wang.
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02016
- source_type: primary
- confidence: medium (methodology paper; abstract does not provide Sharpe / return numbers)
- Key concept: VIX futures term structure modelled as a Markov process; position (long/short) chosen to maximize day-ahead expected utility given current curve shape and level. Functional map fit by a 5-hidden-layer deep neural net. OOS backtest gives "reasonable portfolio performance"; positions are either long or short VIX futures depending on the market environment.
- Implication: a Markov model on the VIX futures curve is a published, peer-style method for generating a conditional signal that sometimes says long, sometimes short. Useful as a "vol regime filter" overlay for any short-vega system. No published Sharpe, so backtest before allocating capital.

### [F2-12] Foundational Markov-switching reference documents the time-varying-parameters family (Song, Woźniak 2020)
- Paper: "Markov Switching" (arXiv:2002.03598, 10 Feb 2020, econ.EM, also DOI: 10.1093/acrefore/9780190625979.013.174 — Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Economics and Finance). Authors: Yong Song, Tomasz Woźniak.
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03598
- source_type: primary (encyclopedia entry by recognised MS-GARCH researcher Woźniak)
- confidence: high
- Key concept: comprehensive taxonomy of MS models — endogenous Markov switching, exogenous Markov switching, infinite HMM, finite mixture, change-point, non-homogeneous MS, time-series analysis, business-cycle analysis. Establishes that "Markov switching" is a family, not a single model.
- Implication: when implementing regime detection, pick the right member of the family for the trading horizon. For an MT5/crypto small account, the simplest useful variant is exogenous Markov switching with macro covariates (Filardo-style TVTP), not the full infinite-HMM machinery.

## Synthesis Notes for the Trader (300-500 USD account on MT5 / crypto)

1. **Regime detection is real, the edge is thin.** MS-GARCH beats GARCH(1,1) on DM test (p=1.28×10⁻⁶) but IC moves only 0.5264 → 0.5371 (Chaudhary 2026). On a $300-500 account, the practical use is **position sizing** by regime, not alpha generation. Source: F2-3.

2. **Calm/Turbulent/Crisis ordering is statistically real on FX (KS p<10⁻¹⁵⁰).** Use this to scale down (or out) in Crisis, scale up in Calm/Turbulent. Source: F2-2.

3. **TVTP is decisive intraday (ΔAIC +500 to +700), not on daily bars.** For an MT5 day-trader, intraday TVTP is the right tool; for an end-of-day swing trader, a static 1D MS-GARCH is sufficient. Source: F2-4.

4. **Short-vega has a published, peer-style academic basis (VRP), but only when actively managed.** Static short-VIX-futures "harvest contango" underperforms dynamic daily-rebalanced strategies (Leung & Ward 2019, explicit negative result). Source: F2-10.

5. **Realized GARCH provides a closed-form VRP** that is empirically the best-fitting model on S&P 500 (Hansen et al. 2021). This is the academic justification for "sell implied > realized" — not a folk strategy but a published econometric finding. Source: F2-9.

6. **Crypto vol needs skewed non-Gaussian GARCH, not vanilla.** Selling crypto options (Deribit, Bybit, OKX) requires explicit skew modelling (Cerqueti et al. 2020). On MT5 (BTCUSD CFD), the limited history and CFD mark-up may make pure short-vol unviable; consider option overlays via Deribit instead. Source: F2-7.

7. **No academic paper found with a published Sharpe ratio for short-vega on a $300-500 retail account.** The Avellaneda 2021 VIX paper gives no numbers; Leung & Ward 2019 shows dynamic beats static but no headline return. The retail "buy options-selling courses" pitch is not backed by a published Sharpe in the arxiv corpus scanned. Source: F2-10, F2-11.

8. **Caveat: residual ARCH remains in MS-GARCH at all FX timescales** (LM=12-38, p=0.000-0.032 in Chaudhary 2026). MS-GARCH is not a closed-form solution — be ready for unmodeled tail events. Source: F2-1 caveat.
